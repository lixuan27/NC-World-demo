The three tables are kept separate because their rows are not mutually comparable: merging them would present a comparison that was never run.

2. Causal versus bidirectional attention

Our strongest controlled evidence. All three arms start from random initialisation, see the same data for the same 7500 steps, and are scored under a byte-identical protocol (32 clips, guidance 1.0, 8 updates per chunk, 1 chunk in flight, cache 4). The only variable is the attention mask and the noise schedule. b is the log-log slope of predicted motion against true motion: 1 is faithful, 0 means the output moves by an amount unrelated to how much the scene actually moved.

Read the first two rows against each other. Assigning each chunk its own noise level is meant to teach the model to denoise a noisy future given a cleaner past, which is exactly the streaming situation. On a bidirectional backbone it makes things 0.4× worse than not doing it at all, because bidirectional attention lets the clean and noisy chunks see each other and the asymmetry the loss was meant to teach never arises. Switching the mask to block-causal makes the same recipe 2.3× better. Causality is not the price paid for streaming; it is the precondition that makes this training signal mean anything.

Two results here are worth stating outright. Longer training context improves FVD by 42% while PSNR stays flat, so the curriculum buys distributional consistency over long horizons rather than per-frame sharpness. And the two sampler knobs move in opposite directions even though their product is what is usually reported as the step count: widening the pipeline costs 2.45× while deepening the denoising helps by 12%. Widening the pipeline means a chunk is committed while the neighbours it depends on are still noisier, which changes the noise level of the history rather than the amount of compute spent.

Autoencoder ceiling: ground-truth video passed through the same frozen codec and scored as if generated reaches 35.38 dB at 253 frames against 36.21 dB at 21 frames — a drop of only 0.83 dB, so codec loss is spatial and does not compound with horizon. Our best model is 19.71 dB, using 56% of the available headroom — the remaining long-horizon artefacts are ours, not the codec's.
